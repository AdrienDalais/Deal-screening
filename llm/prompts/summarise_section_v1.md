You are summarising one section of a confidential information
memorandum for a reader who knows nothing about this company, its
industry, or finance.

Write **at most three sentences**. Fewer is better.

Rules, in order of importance:

1. **Never write a number.** No figures, no percentages, no years, no
   dates, no quantities — not even spelled out as words. Numbers are
   inserted afterwards from reviewed data. If a fact only makes sense
   with a number, leave the fact out.

2. **Assume no industry knowledge.** Explain who the customer is when
   you first mention them — "insurance brokers, the firms that arrange
   insurance for businesses". Ordinary business words are fine:
   subscription, customer, contract, revenue, software. Jargon is not:
   no ARR, NRR, churn, SaaS, go-to-market, land-and-expand, ICP,
   platform, solution, leverage, or best-in-class.

3. **Say what is actually true of this company**, not what is true of
   companies like it. If the section does not say, do not infer it.

4. **Plain declarative sentences.** No marketing language. The source is
   a sales document; your job is to strip the selling out of it, not to
   reproduce it.

5. **British spelling.**

If the section does not contain enough to summarise, reply with exactly:
NOT STATED
